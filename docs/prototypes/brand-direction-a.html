Brand direction A · Ledger / editorial

Abacist

An abacist was a person who reckoned by hand — counting boards, quill, ruled ledgers. This direction borrows the calm of a well-kept account book: warm paper, ink-dark text, a single oxblood rule.

01 Wordmark & mark

Abacist
Abacist
Dashboard · Abacist

The mark is an A made of two pen strokes standing on a double rule — the line a bookkeeper draws under a total once it reconciles. At 16 px the crossbar is dropped and the double rule collapses to one, so it still reads as “A on a line”. The wordmark is set in Libre Caslon Display, a Caslon revival: Caslon was the face of 18th-century ledgers and printed account books.

Why oxblood, not forest green. Ledgers were ruled in red ink, so oxblood is the historically honest accent. Green would also collide head-on with the universal “gain” colour and make every brand surface read as “up”. The catch: oxblood sits near red-for-loss, so --negative is pushed toward a brighter brick-vermilion (#B23A1E), is always paired with a minus sign, and the accent is never used on a figure.

Serif is kept off numbers on purpose: Caslon's old-style figures dance on the baseline and don't align in columns. Inter's tabular lining figures stack digit-for-digit (note the 1s and 8s line up). Negative figures use a true minus (U+2212), not a hyphen.

Brand lives in the chrome: the serif wordmark, the serif page title and italic date, the double rule under the header, the oxblood active tab and primary button. Everything inside the card — figures, the table, the sparkline — is ink, Inter and tabular. The only colour on numbers is semantic gain/loss. The total gets the accountant's double underline.

06 Tagline & voice

Keep a true account.

Proposed tagline. “Account” does double duty: the ledger sense and the honest-telling sense. Voice: a careful bookkeeper — plain, exact, unhurried, never salesy.
